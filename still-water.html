The piece

Still Water opens with a single breath: a rising E-minor arpeggio (B–E–G) answered by a falling sigh, where an F♯ leans against a C-major-7 chord — a quiet lydian shimmer, like light moving on the surface. The B section lifts the melody to its highest note (the B at the 7th fret) and lets it spill back down; the first theme returns more softly, and the piece settles, through one last sigh, onto a ringing open E-minor chord.

Tablature

Thumb plays everything on strings 4–6; fingers i m a take strings 3–1. Let every note ring into the next — lift fingers only when a fret is needed again.

How to practise it

  • Melody alone first (Module 4) — play only the top line with fingers, no thumb, until it sings on its own. The whole tune sits in first position except the three notes at frets 5–7.
  • Bass holds the ground — add the thumb on beat 1 of each bar. Every bass note is a chord root; let it ring for the full bar like a bell.
  • Change on the exhale (Module 3) — every chord change lands on beat 1. Breathe out as you arrive.
  • The rest stroke accent (Module 5) — the emotional peak is the high B opening bar 14. Play it as a rest stroke so it rings above everything; everywhere else, free strokes.
  • Dynamics — A section quiet and even; let B grow naturally as the melody climbs; A′ returns softer than the first time; the coda fades to near-silence before the final rolled chord.
